# Candidate comparison — The One-Line Exit

Judge brief (most important first): (1) a funny one-line resignation letter
whose joke lands; (2) surprise — the punchline arrives somewhere unexpected;
(3) fits the form — one line, no padding, reads like a real letter; (4) a
specific voice — a recognizable job or workplace; (5) quotable, the kind of
line a colleague would forward. Gate: 1–140 characters, single line.

## Candidates

| # | Chars | Line |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | 130 | I hereby resign: after four years of 'per my last email,' I have decided to per my last email myself straight out of this company. |
| 2 | 131 | **I am resigning from IT support: I finally followed my own advice, turned myself off and on again, and discovered I work better off.** |
| 3 | 128 | I resign effective today: the office plant has been watered more consistently than my career, and frankly it got promoted first. |
| 4 | 131 | I hereby resign from the unpaid position of office birthday-card writer, a role I never applied for but performed for eleven years. |
| 5 | 140 | I quit: my two weeks' notice is this sentence, my exit interview is this period, and my reason is the meeting that could have been an email. |
| 6 | 146 | Dear Manager, I resign: I was hired to guard the warehouse at night, but the only thing I ever protected was my sanity, and it is leaving with me. |
| 7 | 98 | I resign: after six years of writing 'as per my last email,' I have decided to send my last email. |
| 8 | 94 | I resign effective immediately: the printer works now, you're welcome, and so do I, elsewhere. |

## Verdict

- **#6 rejected**: 146 characters, over the 140-character gate.
- **#5** is a clever meta-joke but sits exactly on the 140 cap and its three
  parallel clauses dilute the punchline.
- **#1 / #7** share the same "last email" premise; #1 executes it better, but
  the joke is wordplay on office jargon rather than a real resignation reason.
- **#3** is relatable and quotable, but the plant's "promotion" is a familiar
  trope; the voice is generic office rather than a specific job.
- **#4** has the most specific voice (unpaid birthday-card writer) and a good
  turn, but the punchline is a résumé of grievances, not a surprise.
- **#8** is short and punchy, but "so do I, elsewhere" is a weaker landing
  than the setup promises.
- **#2 WINS**: the clearest job voice (IT support), the most surprising turn
  (the standard advice — turn it off and on again — applied to the author
  himself), a punchline that lands in the final words ("I work better off"),
  and the most forwardable line of the eight. 131 characters, one line.
